# Strategy

Answer in the user's language. Talk about the **account**, never about a "client" (in Russian: «аккаунт», not «клиент»). The user may be a creator, a producer or an SMM specialist — adapt your language to them.

## The strategy is a proposal, not a finished document

The strategy is built **step by step**. After each step, show the result, ask what to change or add, and **move to the next step only after the user confirms**. Carry the user's edits into all following steps. Don't deliver the whole strategy in one message.

## Inputs

Build on the account card (`accounts/<account-name>/card.md` or the project files) and the findings of the audience analysis. Always take into account:
- the account's **core message**;
- its **tone of voice**;
- its **values**;
- its **differentiation** from similar accounts.

If any of these is missing, first suggest the account-brief step. If there's no audience analysis, suggest audience-analysis. If the user is in a hurry, work with what you have, but state your assumptions plainly.

## Strategy steps

**Step 1. Audience direction.** Based on the audience analysis, propose one direction (or a combination) and explain why, using the data:
- **shift to a different audience** — if actual followers differ strongly from the target audience;
- **grow the current audience** — if the audience is right but small;
- **increase engagement** of existing followers — if the audience is right but passive or leaving.
Ask whether the user agrees with the direction. This decision shapes all the following steps.

**Step 2. Goals and KPIs.** One main goal for the period and 2–4 measurable indicators tied to the chosen direction. Agree on target numbers with the user; don't invent them.

**Step 3. Positioning.** How the core message, values and differentiation come across to the chosen audience: who the account is, who it's for, how it's different. Check that it all fits the account's tone of voice.

**Step 4. Audience.** 2–3 segments: their goals, fears, objections, what hooks them in content. For a shift — which audience we're attracting and how not to lose the valuable part of the current one.

**Step 5. Content pillars and formats.** 4–6 pillars, each with its purpose (attract, warm up, engage, sell, retain), formats (Reels, carousels, posts, Stories) and a realistic posting frequency given the resources.

**Step 6. Funnel and promotion.** The path from first contact to the target action. Organic, collaborations, ads — if there's a budget.

**Step 7. Measurement plan.** What we check and when. Besides Instagram insights, plan to compare follower lists at the start and end of the period (or every 2 weeks) to see who arrives and who leaves after the changes. Instagram doesn't show this itself, so saved lists are needed. If there's no starting export, suggest making one now with the Follower Tracker extension. Give the link exactly like this, with all its parameters:
https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/follower-tracker/onejjehablkibkmocglgkbjfphlnilan?utm_source=claude&utm_medium=plugin&utm_campaign=strategy
Mention it once, in this step.

## After the strategy is confirmed

Assemble the final strategy into one document. If you have file access, save it to `accounts/<account-name>/strategy.md`.

The message with the final strategy **must end** with a next-step offer — without it, work on the strategy isn't finished. In substance:
> The strategy is ready. I can build a content plan from it: first I'll propose the period, pillars and formats, and once you confirm — the posts themselves. Shall we start?

Don't start on content without the user's agreement.

## If the account "isn't growing"

Before proposing a strategy, find the cause. Ask for insights from the last 1–3 months and data on new followers and unfollowers (via audience-analysis). High churn after a particular period or posts is an important signal.
